# Adobe Stock contributor strategy (what to actually shoot/render)

The single biggest difference between a $20/month contributor and a
$2,000/month contributor is **what they produce, not how skilled they are**.
A mediocre image in a high-demand niche outperforms a beautiful image in
an oversaturated one. This doc tells you what to produce.

> You already have two Adobe Stock accounts. **Use one for stills, one for
> videos.** Adobe Stock's algorithm rewards focused portfolios.

---

## Tier 1 — highest CPM niches in 2025 (where you should spend 80% of your time)

### 1. AI / robotics / "future of work"

The single fastest-growing buyer demand in stock right now. Buyers are
publishers writing AI articles, marketing teams making AI product launches,
and education companies. They need: humanoid robots, abstract neural nets,
glowing data servers, "human + AI" handshakes, futuristic UI mockups.

**Best ways to produce this (no robot needed):**
- Midjourney / DALL·E / Stable Diffusion renders (allowed on Adobe Stock
  via the *Generative AI* contributor category).
- Macro photography of circuit boards, lit at angle with colored gels.
- Screen-capture of code on a laptop, then overlay particle motion in
  After Effects.
- 3D renders in Blender (free) of abstract neural-network meshes.

### 2. Personal finance / business / money

Steady, year-round demand. Editorial outlets, fintech apps, banks, and
finance creators all license heavily.

**What sells:**
- Coins stacked on dark surfaces (shoot 50+ variations in one afternoon).
- Hands counting cash, holding credit cards, signing documents.
- Laptop with spreadsheet/dashboard screenshots (use a mock dashboard
  template).
- Green/red stock-chart B-roll (animate in After Effects in 1 minute each).
- House keys + dollar bills + calculator on a wood desk (mortgage concept).

### 3. Sustainable / green living

Demand has tripled in 2 years and is now a top-10 search category.

**What sells:**
- Solar panels close-ups, wind turbines, EV charging.
- Reusable water bottles, bamboo cutlery, glass containers.
- Vegetable gardens, compost bins, urban farming.
- Recycling symbols, circular-economy infographics.

### 4. Diverse, real-feeling people (lifestyle)

Adobe's #1 search term over the last 4 years is variations of "real people".
Stock buyers are sick of corporate handshakes. They want unposed, slightly
imperfect, candid lifestyle shots.

**What sells:**
- Hands only (no faces — easier, no model release headaches).
- Over-the-shoulder shots of someone working/cooking/typing.
- Diverse-age and diverse-ethnicity groups (you can hire 2 friends + buy
  pizza, get 200 sellable shots in 2 hours).

---

## Tier 2 — medium demand, lower competition (where you should spend 20%)

- Tech objects (phones, laptops, smart-home devices on minimalist backgrounds).
- "Education / learning" — books, notebooks, online learning setups.
- Wellness / mental health — meditation, yoga, journaling.
- Cooking / food — overhead, well-lit, "real home cooking" not restaurant.

## Tier 3 — avoid (oversaturated, low CPM)

- Generic landscapes (mountains, beaches, sunsets) unless you're a professional photographer.
- Pets (oversaturated).
- Generic city skylines.
- "Stock photo of a woman laughing alone with salad" — meme-tier, no buyers.

## Production cadence (this is what makes the income compound)

| Frequency | Target |
|---|---|
| Daily | 5 keywords-researched, ready-to-shoot ideas in a notes file |
| 3× per week | A 2-hour shoot OR render session producing **10+ sellable assets** |
| Weekly | 50 assets uploaded with proper metadata (use `prep.py`) |
| Monthly | 200 new assets in the portfolio |
| Yearly | ~2,000 new assets — total portfolio grows past 5,000 |

A 5,000-asset portfolio in the right niches will reliably earn $1,500–$5,000/month passively.

## Niches that pair perfectly with the rest of this repo

The `shorts/` pipeline produces personal-finance video content. Repurpose
the same on-screen overlays as PNGs you upload to Adobe Stock:

1. After rendering a Short, open `shorts/output/<date>/<slug>/_work/bg.png`.
2. Use the on-screen overlay text from `script.json` to create 4–5 caption
   variants in Canva ($20-billion concept, save more, etc).
3. Save each as a 4000×6000 PNG.
4. Upload to Adobe Stock as a vertical-format "infographic" — these sell
   surprisingly well to Instagram-marketing-team buyers.

You're now monetizing the same asset **3 different ways**:

1. The MP4 → YouTube Shorts / Reels / Fiverr delivery.
2. The PNG infographics → Adobe Stock.
3. The script.md → finance-niche newsletter content (next module).

## Adobe Stock contributor tips that are NOT obvious

- **Only the first 10 keywords are weighted heavily.** Spend more time on
  keyword 1-10 than on 11-49.
- **Title is searched too** — buyers often search the same phrase that's
  in your title. Don't write generic "businessman in office"; write
  "young Asian businessman reviewing quarterly numbers at 7am office".
- **Categories matter for filtering.** Pick the most specific one. If your
  image fits "Business" AND "Lifestyle", pick whichever has fewer competing
  uploads that day.
- **Reject rate is a feature, not a bug.** Adobe rejects ~15% of submissions
  to maintain quality. If your rejection rate is >40%, your titles or
  noise/focus are off — not the niche.
- **AI-generated content must be declared.** Adobe accepts AI but only in
  the Generative AI category; if you don't declare it, you get banned.
- **Model releases:** Hands-only photos don't need them, identifiable
  faces do. Use Adobe's free e-signature release if you involve people.
